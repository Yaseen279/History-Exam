Block 1 · The study on one page

What we studied

Question: in adults in Baghdad, is health anxiety linked to cyberchondria, and how do online and AI searching shape what people do before seeing a doctor?

Design: analytical cross-sectional online survey (your Google Form), Arabic and English, July–September 2026, convenience and snowball sampling, n = 300, no missing data, one response per Google account.

Two scales you put in the form:

  • SHAI-14 (health anxiety): 14 items scored 0–3, total 0–42. ≥ 18 = “elevated” (screening, not a diagnosis).
  • CSS-12 (cyberchondria): 12 items scored 1–5, total 12–60, four 3-item dimensions: excessiveness, compulsion, distress, reassurance seeking.

We surveyed 300 adults in Baghdad online, using the SHAI-14 for health anxiety and the CSS-12 for cyberchondria.

Block 3 · The statistics you must explain

Why each test was chosen (one rule)

The type of variable decides the test:

You compare…Test
a score between 2 groupsMann–Whitney U
a score between 3+ groupsKruskal–Wallis H
two categories (yes/no × group)χ² (Fisher if small)
two scoresPearson r (+ Spearman)
a score from many factorsmultiple linear regression
a yes/no from many factorsbinary logistic regression
a path through a middle variablemediation + Sobel test

Our scores were not normally distributed, so we compared groups with non-parametric tests, and all analyses were done in SPSS 26 with p < 0.05.

Block 3 · The statistics you must explain

The tests (1 of 5)

p-value

The chance of a result this strong if there were really no effect. Below 0.05 = significant. It says nothing about size.

Ours: r = 0.47, p < 0.001

95% confidence interval

The range where the true value probably lies. For an odds ratio, if it includes 1 → not significant.

Ours: r 95% CI 0.38–0.55, always above 0

Mean ± SD / median (IQR)

Average and spread; median and IQR when data are skewed.

Ours: SHAI 12.3 ± 5.8; median 12 (8–16)

Block 3 · The statistics you must explain

The tests (2 of 5)

Shapiro–Wilk

Tests whether scores are normally distributed. Ours were not (p < 0.01), so groups were compared with non-parametric tests.

Ours: SHAI and CSS p < 0.01

Cronbach α

Do the items of a scale measure the same thing? ≥ 0.7 acceptable, ≥ 0.8 good.

Ours: SHAI 0.81, CSS 0.86

Mann–Whitney U / Kruskal–Wallis H

Compare a score between 2 groups / 3 or more groups without assuming normality.

Ours: Chronic condition: SHAI higher, p = 0.004

Block 3 · The statistics you must explain

The tests (3 of 5)

Chi-square (χ²) / Fisher / Cramér’s V

Is a yes/no outcome related to a category? Fisher when cells are small. V = strength (0.1 small, 0.3 medium).

Ours: 52.6% vs 23.0% high CSS, OR 3.71

Pearson r / Spearman ρ / partial r

Strength of a link between two scores; Spearman uses ranks (checks Pearson); partial r removes other variables.

Ours: r 0.47, ρ 0.47, partial 0.48

Multiple linear regression, B, β, R²

Predicts a score from many factors at once. B = change per unit; β = comparable strength; R² = share explained; hierarchical = blocks in order.

Ours: SHAI β 0.48; R² 0.317

Block 3 · The statistics you must explain

The tests (4 of 5)

Binary logistic regression, AOR

Predicts a yes/no outcome. Adjusted odds ratio: 1 = no effect, > 1 higher odds, < 1 lower.

Ours: AOR 3.12 per SD of CSS

Hosmer–Lemeshow, Nagelkerke R², AUC

Logistic model checks: H–L p > 0.05 = good fit; Nagelkerke ≈ R²; AUC = how well it separates groups (0.8+ good).

Ours: H–L p 0.50, Nagelkerke 0.31, AUC 0.83

Mediation + Sobel test

Does part of X → Y pass through M? Paths a (X→M) and b (M→Y); indirect = a × b; Sobel z tests if it is above zero (|z| > 1.96).

Ours: a 0.46 × b 0.35 = 0.16, z 5.14

Block 3 · The statistics you must explain

The tests (5 of 5)

z-scores

Scores converted so mean = 0 and SD = 1, so effects are “per 1 SD” and comparable.

Ours: AOR 3.12 per 1 SD (≈ 8 CSS points)

VIF

Checks that predictors are not near-duplicates; below 5 is fine.

Ours: all low

Block 3 · The statistics you must explain

Quiz 3

  1. Why Mann–Whitney and not the t-test?

    Scores were not normal (Shapiro–Wilk p < 0.01)

  2. What does AOR 0.61 for age mean?

    Each older age group has lower odds: protective

  3. What does a Hosmer–Lemeshow p of 0.50 mean?

    The model fits well (we want p > 0.05)

  4. How is the Sobel z calculated?

    a×b / √(b²SEa² + a²SEb²); |z| > 1.96 → p < 0.05

  5. What is β for?

    To compare the strength of predictors on one scale

Block 5 · Questions the committee will ask

Questions and answers (1 of 4)

You made the questionnaire. Why these two scales?

The SHAI-14 and the CSS-12 are short, validated and widely used, so our results can be compared with other countries; the CSS-12 keeps the four dimensions of the long scale.

Why is the sample mostly young medical students?

Convenience and snowball sampling through our own networks; we say percentages cannot be generalised, but associations are still informative.

How did you stop people answering twice?

The form accepted one response per Google account, and no duplicate patterns were found.

Why SHAI ≥ 18?

It best separated people with health anxiety from healthy controls in the Swedish validation study; we also show 22 and 29.

Block 5 · Questions the committee will ask

Questions and answers (2 of 4)

Did you translate the scales formally?

No formal back-translation; that is a limitation. Reliability was good and Arabic and English scores did not differ.

Which software?

IBM SPSS Statistics version 26, with a syntax file that reproduces every table.

Why did you not correct for multiple testing?

We used the standard p < 0.05 and state it as a limitation; the main results have p < 0.001.

Explain r = 0.47 to a patient.

People who worry more about illness tend to search more and more anxiously; worry explains about a fifth of the differences.

Block 5 · Questions the committee will ask

Questions and answers (3 of 4)

Why is your r lower than the meta-analyses (0.62)?

Low and narrow health anxiety, confident internet users and the short 12-item scale all weaken correlations.

Does this prove a vicious cycle?

No: one time point, and the reverse order fits too. It is compatible with a cycle.

Why did the AI effect disappear?

It depended on 32 people who named a chatbot without ticking AI; with the strict definition p = 0.21. Only 34 were non-users.

What is the clinical message?

Ask every patient, without judgement, what they searched or asked an AI.

Block 5 · Questions the committee will ask

Questions and answers (4 of 4)

Why non-parametric tests but linear regression?

Groups were compared non-parametrically because scores were skewed; regression residuals were close to normal and n = 300 is large, and Spearman confirmed Pearson.

What is the most important limitation?

Cross-sectional design: we cannot tell which comes first.
